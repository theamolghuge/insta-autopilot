Apple is tightening Full Disk Access on Macs, warning that AI agents make the setting riskier

Apple says it will add new controls to Full Disk Access, the macOS permission that lets an app see a user's files, mail, messages and even browsing history.

The setting was built so backup apps could work properly. Apple says some developers now use it in ways that expose everything on a Mac without users fully understanding it.

Going forward, granting an app that level of access will require "very explicit user action," according to a post on Apple's developer site. Apple did not give a timeline.

The company tied the change directly to AI: as agents become more capable and autonomous, it says the risks of this kind of access will grow substantially.

The move follows an Inc. columnist's report that Meta's Muse agent on Mac knew the content of his private messages, a claim Meta disputed, and a Wired report on a flaw in ChatGPT's Mac app that could have exposed sensitive data.
